1. Quick start checklist

  1. Open the site on every device over the internet (https). Use the rear 1× camera on each phone.
  2. Setup → Connect phones: on the device that will show the 3D view and games, tap Create room (Phone A), then Share… the link. On each other phone, open the link and tap Join room. They become Phone B and Phone C in join order.
  3. Setup → Start cameras: choose Body only (fastest) or Body + detailed hands on Phone A — the choice is sent to the others — then tap Enable camera + skeleton on every phone.
  4. Put the phones on stands, upright (portrait), aimed at the same spot about chest high, 45–90° apart. A third phone works best on the other side. Move each phone back until its setup check shows ✓ framing good.
  5. Setup → Align cameras (on Phone A): enter the player's height, tap Start alignment, and walk slowly around for ~10 s — toward and away, side to side, arms up and out. Look for Aligned — Good.
  6. Open 3D view, Cubes or Ping pong. Don't move the phones; if you do, align again.
No phones handy? Setup → Run demo simulates a person and two or three virtual phones — see Testing without cameras.

1b. Testing without cameras (demo person)

Their "camera" data goes through exactly the same steps as real phones (matching, alignment, 3D), so everything downstream — 3D view, costumes, Playhouse, effects, flankers, theremin, gestures, TV display — can be tried on one computer or tablet with no cameras at all.

  2. Alignment starts by itself; the person walks around for about 10 seconds until it says Aligned — Good.
  3. Choose what the person does with Demo person does in Setup, or from the small menu that appears at the top of every tab while the demo runs.

📂 Movement files

A movement file is a recorded or pre-made routine that the demo person performs instead of one of the built-in motions — so you can replay a performance without anyone in front of the cameras.

  • 🥁 Load the drummer (Setup, under “Demo person does”) loads the built-in 48-second Drum performance: a ready pose, a count-in on the hi-hat, a rock groove, a ride-cymbal groove, tribal toms, open hi-hats, fills and a big ending with the sticks in the air. Then open Drums and press Play drums to hear it. Even quicker: Drums → ▶ Watch a drum performance does all of that in one tap.
  • 🎵 Load the thereminist loads the built-in Theremin recital (about 2 minutes, four public-domain tunes: Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star, Beethoven's Ode to Joy, Amazing Grace and When the Saints Go Marching In). It also sets the theremin to the settings it was made for (Classic, Major scale, key G, octave 3, range 1 octave). Then open Theremin and press Play — or just tap Theremin → ▶ Watch a theremin recital, which does everything in one go. The current song's name shows at the top of the theremin screen.
  • 📂 Load movement file… loads any movement file (.json), including your own 3D recordings saved from the 3D view (● Record, then JSON) — record yourself once, play it back any time.
  • The routine loops. Choose another motion to stop it, or 📂 File in the top menu to go back to it. It starts from the top after the cameras finish aligning.

“That file has no movement frames” / no body

The file must be a Motion Lab movement file or a 3D recording (JSON, not CSV) with the hips and shoulders tracked.

🎵 The Raven soundtrack

“Raven things” has its own music: a dark-but-playful 35-second loop (plucked strings, harpsichord, cello, woodblocks and a few raven caws) written on the same beat as the raven's moves, so they line up. Footsteps land on the beat while it struts, each wingbeat gets a whoosh and a drum, the cello sings while it soars, hops go “boing” and land with a thump, the music stops for each caw, and every peck gets a click.

  1. Tick 🎵 Raven soundtrack in Setup (under “Demo person does”), or tap 🔇 Music in the bar at the top while the demo runs. This also switches the demo person to Raven things and starts the routine and the song together from the top.
  2. Turn your device's sound up; use Music volume to balance it.
  3. Tap 🎵 On to turn it off. It also stops by itself if you choose a different motion or stop the demo, and waits while the cameras are aligning.

No music

Check the device isn't on silent (iPhone: the switch on the side) and the volume is up. The music only plays during Raven things. If the status line says the soundtrack couldn't be loaded, the music folder is missing from the site — see Updating the site.

The music seems slightly ahead of or behind the moves

The music follows the demo's clock and allows for the short delay before each pose reaches the screen. Bluetooth speakers and headphones add their own delay (often 0.1–0.3 s), so the music can sound a little late on them; wired or built-in speakers line up best.

Does the TV play the music?

No — the TV receives the picture only. The music plays on the device running the demo; connect that device to a speaker near the TV.

The person is just walking, not dancing

While alignment is collecting, the person always walks (alignment needs lots of different positions). The chosen motion starts as soon as it says Aligned. A running game also takes over until it ends.

I don't see the menu at the top

It only appears while the demo is running. On a phone it replaces the "Demo" chip.

2. Requirements

  • Browsers: Safari or Chrome on iPhone/iPad (on Apple devices every browser uses Safari's engine, so they behave the same); Chrome on Android; Chrome, Edge or Safari on a computer.
  • Secure address: the camera only works on an https:// address (Netlify provides this) or on localhost. Opening index.html as a file won't allow the camera.
  • Internet access to these services (school or office networks sometimes block them):
    • unpkg.com — PeerJS connection library
    • cdn.jsdelivr.net — MediaPipe tracking models (~10–25 MB the first time, then cached)
    • 0.peerjs.com — the public "signalling" server that introduces phones to each other
    • Direct phone-to-phone WebRTC traffic (some networks block it — see Connecting phones).
  • Devices: recent phones/tablets work best. Older devices may track at under 8 frames per second (the setup check will say so).

4. Connecting phones

"PeerJS library did not load. Disable content blockers or try another network."

Why: the connection library from unpkg.com was blocked (content blocker, private relay, or a filtered network).

Fix: turn off content blockers for the site, reload; or switch to mobile data / a phone hotspot.

"Room error: ID "motion-lab-…-host" is taken" (or similar)

Why: that room code is still registered — usually from a previous session on the same code that hasn't timed out.

Fix: clear the Room code box and tap Create room again to get a fresh random code, or wait about a minute.

Join phone stays on "Connecting to Phone A…" or shows "Could not connect to peer"

Why: Phone A's room isn't open (page reloaded, screen locked, wrong code), or the network blocks direct phone-to-phone WebRTC.

Fix: on Phone A make sure the status says "Room ready"; check the code matches; keep Phone A's screen on. If it still fails, put all devices on the same phone hotspot — hotspots almost never block WebRTC.

"This room already has three phones (A, B and C)."

Fix: the limit is three. A phone that disconnected frees its letter; the next phone to join takes it.

A phone keeps disconnecting

Why: phones pause web pages when the screen locks or the browser goes to the background.

Fix: keep every screen on (iPhone: Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock › Never while testing; Android: Developer options › Stay awake while charging). Keep the site in the foreground. Rejoin with the same link — it gets a letter again.

Phone A was reloaded

Reloading the host closes the room. Create the room again (same code is fine after a minute), and rejoin from the other phones. A saved alignment can be restored with Load saved if the phones haven't moved.

5. Cameras & tracking

"Camera or library error: NotAllowedError…" / no permission prompt

Fix: allow camera access for the site. iPhone/iPad: Settings › Safari (or Chrome) › Camera › Allow, or the "aA" menu › Website Settings. Android Chrome: tap the lock icon by the address › Permissions › Camera. Then reload.

"Camera or library error: NotReadableError" / black preview

Why: another app or tab is using the camera.

Fix: close other camera apps and tabs (including another Motion Lab tab), then tap Enable camera again.

"First create or join a room."

The camera starts only after the phone has a role. Create or join first. (In demo mode the camera is not used.)

Stuck on "loading skeleton tracking…", or "Unable to load …/pose.js" / "MediaPipe unavailable"

Why: the tracking model downloads from cdn.jsdelivr.net (larger for detailed hands). Slow or blocked networks stall here.

Fix: wait 20–30 s on first use; disable content blockers; try another network. Once loaded it is cached.

"Camera active — step back to show full body"

The tracker can't find a person. Make sure the whole body is in view, the room is reasonably lit, and only one person is in frame (the tracker follows one person).

Fingers not tracked

Choose Body + detailed hands on Phone A (it applies to all phones). Hands must be within about 2 m and visible to at least two phones. The phone's stats line shows "+ 2 hands" when both are seen.

Left and right swap briefly

When a camera sees the person from behind or exactly side-on, the tracker can confuse left and right. Keep every phone seeing at least part of the person's front, within ~90° of the others. With three phones, a disagreeing view is automatically ignored for that joint.

6. Setup-check warnings (under each phone's preview)

7. Alignment

Alignment works out where the phones are relative to each other using the person's own joints — no checkerboard needed. It assumes the phones don't move afterwards and keep the same orientation.

"Not enough shared joints (N). Make sure both phones can see the whole body."

Fix: every phone must see the person at the same time during the walk-around. Check framing warnings, and that all phones show "connected" with skeletons drawn.

"Calibration failed — try again while moving around more of the space." / "Could not see shoulders and hips together in both cameras."

Fix: move more: step toward and away from the cameras and side to side, raise arms. Standing still gives the solver nothing to work with. Make sure shoulders and hips are visible to Phones A and B.

Result says "Poor — redo with more movement", or "% of joints agree" is low

Typical causes, most likely first: a phone moved; cameras too far apart in angle (one sees mostly your back); moving too fast during the walk-around; one device is very slow; the lens setting is far off. Fix the cause and align again. As a guide: Good < 3 px error, OK 3–7 px, Poor > 7 px. "Joints agree" above ~80% is healthy.

"Phone C not used: …"

Phone C's alignment failed or it didn't see the person at the same time as Phone B ("Phone C was seen in too few frames" / "Phone C needs to see the person at the same time as Phone B"). Two-phone tracking continues. Fix C's framing and align again.

The 3D skeleton stops after rotating a phone

Alignment is tied to each phone's picture size. Turning a phone between portrait and landscape changes it, so that phone is dropped from 3D. Turn it back or align again.

Lens field of view

Leave it on iPhone 1× (67°) unless you know otherwise; testing showed most phones/tablets are close enough. Never use the 0.5× ultra-wide lens (too much distortion). If one device has an unusual lens and alignment stays poor, try Wider (75°) or Narrower (60°).

"Load saved" / "No saved alignment on this phone yet."

Each successful alignment is saved on Phone A. "Load saved" restores it — only valid if no phone moved or rotated since.

Aim point messages

After aligning, the status shows where Phones A and B's lines of sight cross ("aim point") and its height. "Cameras A and B are pointing in the same direction" or "…cross behind a camera" means the phones aren't aimed at a shared spot — re-aim and realign. Tips appear if the point is too high or low for comfortable reaching (best ≈ 0.6–0.7 × the player's height).

8. 3D view

"Waiting for both phones to see the person…"

At least two aligned phones must see the person at the same moment. Check each phone's preview and the "cameras live" chip.

Skeleton is jittery or limbs change length

Improve framing (setup check), lighting and device speed, and realign. Three phones reduce jitter. Some wobble (a few cm) is normal for camera-based tracking.

Sizes look wrong

Scale comes from the Person's height entered in Setup. Correct it — the change applies immediately.

Costume looks odd

Costumes need shoulders and hips; face details need the nose and ears. Partly hidden bodies give partly drawn costumes.

Where did the buttons on the picture go?

The buttons on top of each display (Full screen, Exit, View, 360°, Snapshot, Cycle characters…) fade away after 10 seconds so they don't cover the picture. Touch the picture (or move the mouse over it) and they come back. The touch that brings them back never presses a button by accident, so tap once to show them and again to choose.

Full screen doesn't fill the screen on iPhone

iPhone Safari doesn't allow true full screen for web pages, so the view fills the browser window instead. Turn the phone sideways for more room. Tap ✕ Exit to leave.

9. Cube game

Can't reach some cubes

With Cubes centred: Where Phones A & B aim, the block sits where the phones point. Aim them at chest height, or switch to In front of the player.

"Can't see your shoulders in 3D yet — face the cameras."

The game waits until your body is tracked in 3D. Face Phone A with shoulders visible to two phones.

Hand is inside the cube but it won't select

You must hold for the chosen time (0.5 s default). Leaving the cube restarts the timer (a small grace margin absorbs jitter). Use detailed hands for fingertip precision. Targets are skipped after 15 s.

No sound

On iPhone, the ring/silent switch mutes web audio. Sound starts after you tap Start.

10. Ping pong

No paddle appears

The paddle follows the chosen Hand; that hand's wrist and knuckles must be tracked by two phones. If tracking stalls for over 0.7 s the paddle disappears until it's back.

The table is in an awkward place

It appears in front of where you stand when the countdown ends, facing the way you face. Stop the match, stand where you want your end of the table facing Phone A, and start again.

I keep missing the ball

Start with Shot assist: Strong, Game speed: 60%, Opponent: Easy, Paddle: Big, tracking Body only. Watch the ball's shadow on the table to judge height. The Tracking delay stat should be under ~150 ms; higher means a slow device or network.

Shots fly wildly

Use Strong assist (it steers shots onto the table) and Paddle angle: Auto. "From hand" uses your real wrist angle, which is noisy with camera tracking.

Serving

On your serve, a ball floats above your end — swing forward through it. The robot serves on its turns. Serves alternate every two points (every point at 10–10).

10b. Playhouse

Built-in backdrops

Every character comes with its own picture: Skeleton — a motion-capture studio; Snowman — a snowy village at dusk; Dragon — a volcanic land with a ruined castle; Human skeleton — a moonlit graveyard; Witch — an autumn witch's cottage; the gymnasts — a gymnastics arena. Raven — a ring of standing stones on a misty hilltop under a full moon. They're part of the site, so every new user sees them straight away and they're used when cycling characters. Your own uploads always take their place. Remove on a built-in picture turns it off (painted curtain stage) for that character; ↺ Use built-in picture brings it back — also handy if you uploaded your own and want the original again.

3D characters (VRM)

You can bring in your own rigged 3D characters in VRM format. In the Playhouse, open the 3D characters (VRM) card and tap Import VRM…. The character then appears in every character list (Playhouse, backup dancers, Drums) and follows your body, head, hands and fingers, with hair and clothes that swing and eyes that blink. It is sized to your leg length so the feet land where yours do.

Where to get characters: make your own in VRoid Studio (free, Windows/Mac — export as VRM), or download one from VRoid Hub (look for models whose author allows downloads). Both VRM 0.x and VRM 1.0 files work. Two sample characters are built in: VRoid girl (sample) by pixiv Inc. and Seed-san © VirtualCast, Inc. (VRM Public License 1.0, credit shown on stage).

Good to know: imported characters are stored on that device only (import on each phone/tablet you use). Each VRM file says what its author allows — check before performing with it in public. Files are often 10–30 MB, so the first load takes a few seconds. 3D characters are drawn in the Playhouse and Drums; the 3D view and other games show the stick figure. Faces don't follow your expression (only blinking). If your arms look shorter or wider apart than the character's, that's normal — the character copies the direction of your limbs, not their length.

Character songs

In the Playhouse, each character can have its own song (the Character song card). It starts when you open the Playhouse with that character, or switch to them, and loops; switching character switches song, and it stops when you leave the Playhouse. Pinocchio comes with I’m a Real Boy! built in. To give any character a song, choose them, tap Upload song… and pick an MP3/M4A/WAV — uploaded songs are kept on that device only. Remove takes a song away; Use built-in song brings Pinocchio’s back. If nothing plays, tap ▶ Play song once — phones only allow sound after a tap. On iPhone/iPad the volume slider has no effect; use the side buttons. The Raven demo soundtrack takes priority while it is playing.

Pinocchio's nose

Pinocchio is the wooden puppet from Carlo Collodi's 1883 book: jointed wooden limbs, a suit of flowered paper, shoes of bark and a soft pointed cap of bread dough. Touch your nose and his nose grows; let go and it slowly shrinks back. When it gets long, leaves sprout on it and a little bird lands on the end. (With backup dancers on, every Pinocchio's nose grows together.) He is drawn as a realistic lit 3D model — a varnished, carved wooden marionette with a sculpted face, glossy painted eyes that blink, brass joint pins, soft shadows and fabric textures. The 3D renderer (three.js) is downloaded the first time you pick him; if the device is offline or can't run WebGL he falls back to the simpler painted puppet automatically. On older phones the 3D version can be heavier — pick another character if the frame rate drops. He comes with a built-in backdrop and 360° panorama of Geppetto's workshop in golden evening light (the prompts used are in the backdrop section, if you want to make your own).

A built-in backdrop doesn't appear (painted stage instead)

The pictures load from the site's backdrops folder a moment after the page opens. On a very slow connection give it a few seconds. If they never appear, the backdrops folder wasn't included when the site was updated — see Updating the site. Opening index.html straight from a computer's files (not from the website) can also stop them loading.

Uploading a backdrop

Pick the character, tap Upload backdrop… and choose a picture. It replaces the built-in picture for that character on this device only. Each character keeps its own backdrop; tick "Save as the default…" first to set one picture for every character without its own. Pictures are shrunk to about 1600 px so they fit in the browser's storage.

"That file couldn't be opened as a picture."

Use JPG or PNG. iPhone HEIC photos open in Safari but not in every browser on a computer — export or screenshot them as JPG.

"…storage is full, so it won't be remembered after reloading."

The browser's storage for this site is full. Remove backdrops you no longer use (choose the character, tap Remove), then upload again.

Feet float above or sink into the floor

Drag Floor line to where the floor in the picture meets the back wall or horizon. Adjust Character size to suit the picture's scale, and Sideways to place the character.

Character walks off the picture, or moves the "wrong" way

Tick Keep the character centred to follow the performer. Mirror (on by default) makes the character move the same way you do, like a mirror; untick it for an audience view.

Flanking characters (backup dancers)

Only one person is tracked, so flanking characters are backup dancers: up to two extra characters who copy your movements, standing a step toward the back of the stage — about a metre behind you, one on each side — so they look a little smaller and are drawn behind the main character.

Setting them up

  1. Open Playhouse and choose the main Character as usual.
  2. In the Flanking characters card, choose who stands On the left and On the right (left and right as you see them on the screen). Each list has None, Same as main, and every character. Use one side only for a single flanker.
  3. Pick a Timing and Spacing, and optionally tick Flankers mirror you.
  4. Step into view — the flankers appear with you.
SettingWhat it does
On the left / On the rightWhich character stands on each side. Same as main follows the main character (it changes when the main character cycles); any other choice stays fixed.
Timing: In syncFlankers move exactly with you.
Timing: Echo · ¼ s / ½ sFlankers repeat your moves a quarter or half second later, like a canon or an echo.
Timing: RippleThe movement flows across the line like a wave: the left flanker moves first (right along with you), your main character follows one step later, then the right flanker one step after that. Nothing can move before you do, so the main character is the one that waits.
Ripple stepTime between each character in the ripple: 0.2 s (quick), 0.3 s (default), 0.45 s (slow, very visible). Shown when Ripple is chosen.
Ripple directionLeft → right (default) or right → left.
SpacingHow far each flanker stands to the side of you (0.6–1.8 m). Smaller keeps everyone on screen; larger spreads the line out.
Flankers mirror youFlankers move as your mirror image (raise your right arm, they raise their left) — a symmetrical chorus-line look.

Your choices are remembered on this device. Flankers also appear in Snapshot pictures, in full screen, on the TV, and while Cycle characters is running (they keep their own characters; the backdrop follows the main character).

Ideas: three witches in a ripple for a spell-casting dance; a snowman with two snowmen (Same as main) echoing ½ s behind; two gymnasts mirroring you for a floor routine.

A flanker is cut off at the edge of the picture

Reduce Spacing, lower Character size, or tick Keep the character centred so the whole line follows you. Walking toward one side of the room moves the whole line too.

Flankers overlap me or each other

Increase Spacing. Wide characters (the Dragon's wings and tail, the Witch's robe) need more room — try 1.3 m or more.

The flankers don't appear

Check the side isn't set to None. Flankers only appear while you're being tracked (the main character is showing). With an echo or ripple they appear a moment after you step in. The picture only updates while the Playhouse is on screen — scroll back up if you changed settings further down the page.

They're on the "wrong" side, or move the opposite way

Left and right are as you see them on the screen. If you've unticked Mirror (audience view), your own movements appear reversed and the flankers follow suit. If they move the opposite way from you, untick Flankers mirror you.

The ripple looks like everyone moves together

Choose a longer Ripple step (0.45 s) and make big, clear movements — a sweep of the arm or a step to the side shows the wave best.

The echo feels too late or too early

Try the other echo length, or switch to In sync. Camera tracking itself adds ~0.1–0.2 s for every character, so even "in sync" is very slightly behind real life.

360° panorama (the camera circles the character)

Instead of standing in front of a flat picture, the character stands in the middle of a 360° panorama and the camera slowly walks a circle round them. You see the character from the front, the side, the back and the other side, and the whole scene turns behind them.

Using it

  1. Open Playhouse and tap 🌐 360° on the picture (or set Stage view to 360° in the 360° panorama card). Tap ▭ Flat to go back.
  2. Step into view. The character stays in the centre of the screen and follows you round the room.
  3. Drag the picture left or right to turn it yourself (choose Don't move to turn it only by hand).
SettingWhat it does

DirectionAnticlockwise or clockwise.
View widthHow much of the scene you see at once (55°–120°, default 90°). Wider shows more scenery and makes a small panorama look sharper; narrower feels closer.
Horizon in the pictureWhere the horizon (far edge of the ground) is in your panorama, from the top. Move it until the character's feet sit on the ground instead of floating in the sky or sinking — usually 50–60%.
Character size(In the Stage card.) In 360° view this moves the camera closer or further away.
Upload panorama… / RemoveEach character can have its own panorama. The Snowman (snowy village) and the Raven (moonlit stone circle) come with built-in panoramas. Characters without one use their backdrop, mirrored round into a seamless circle (or a painted studio if there's no backdrop). ↺ Use built-in panorama brings the built-in one back.

Effects, flanking characters, snowfall, Snapshot, full screen, Cycle characters and the TV all work in 360° view. Flanking characters really are standing around you, so as the camera goes round, the one nearest the camera passes in front — the camera keeps a little further back when flankers are on so nobody bumps into the lens.

Making a panorama: ask an image generator for "a seamless 360-degree equirectangular panorama of …, empty flat ground all around, no people, horizon across the middle", as wide as it allows (at least 3:1, ideally 4096 pixels or wider). The Playhouse blends the left and right edges together so the join is soft.
360° panorama — template
Seamless 360-degree equirectangular panorama, 2:1 or wider, of [YOUR SCENE], eye-level camera at 1.2 m, horizon running straight across the middle of the image, wide empty flat open ground all around the viewer with nothing close to the camera, no people, no characters, no text, soft even lighting, highly detailed

The character floats above the ground or sinks into it

Move Horizon in the picture. If the panorama has a lot of sky, the horizon is lower (bigger number).

The panorama looks blurry

It's being stretched to go all the way round. Use a wider panorama (4096 pixels or more), or widen View width.

I can see a join or a jump in the scenery

The picture's left and right edges don't match. The Playhouse softens the join, but a true "seamless 360°" panorama looks best. Mirrored backdrops always join perfectly but show the scene twice (once flipped).

Dragging scrolls the page instead of turning the picture

Drag on the picture itself. In 360° view the picture catches drags; scroll with the space around it.

Right/left are the character's own. With Mirror unticked (audience view) the character faces you, so its right hand is on your left of the screen. Choose the other side, or Both.

On iPhone/iPad the picture opens a share sheet — choose Save Image. Some in-app browsers block downloads; open the site in Safari or Chrome.

10d. Duck shoot

Setting up

  1. Setup: choose Body + detailed hands (the thumb trigger needs finger tracking), start the cameras and align as usual. Stand 1.5–2.5 m from the phones so your hand is big enough to track; your gun hand must be visible to at least two phones.
  2. Show it on the TV: in Control → TV display, open the TV link in the TV's web browser (smart-TV browser, Fire TV Silk, a laptop or computer connected to the TV…). The TV joins as a display only — it doesn't use a camera slot. Click once on the TV (or press OK on the remote) to turn on sound. Alternatively, mirror Phone A's screen to the TV and use Full screen.
  3. Calibrate aim: in Control → Pointing at the TV, stand where you'll play, tap Calibrate aim, and for each of the 5 targets on the TV point your finger gun at it and fire. This teaches the app where your TV is. Enter the rough TV distance first. Quick aim is a shortcut: point at the middle of the TV and tap it (less accurate; uses the TV width setting).
  4. Start game. Ducks fly up in waves; you have 3 shots per wave. Hit enough of each round's 10 ducks to reach the next round.
How aiming works: the app draws a line from between your eyes through your index fingertip — the same way you'd naturally aim — and finds where it meets the TV. Look along your finger at the target.

The gun won't fire

Check the Trigger card readout in the Duck shoot tab. Thumb gap should drop below the "fire" number when your thumb touches your index finger, and go well above it with the thumb up. If it says "needs detailed hands", switch tracking in Setup. If it never drops low enough, raise Trigger sensitivity; if it fires on its own, lower it. Make sure your thumb is visible to the cameras (not hidden behind your hand). As a fallback choose Fire with: Thumb or hold steady — holding the aim still for ~0.7 s fires too.

It fires twice / on its own

Lower Trigger sensitivity. After each shot the thumb must come back up before the next shot can fire.

The crosshair is offset or jumps around

Recalibrate from where you're standing — calibration is tied to your position. Keep your head still and look along your finger. If it's still jumpy, try Aim by: Along the finger, improve framing/lighting so the hand is tracked well, or use a third phone. A dim crosshair means your hand isn't currently tracked.

Shots land slightly off target

The shot uses where you were aiming a split second before your thumb dropped (pressing the thumb jerks the hand). Lead fast ducks a little, choose Hit area: Generous, or set Ducks per wave: 1.

The TV says "Waiting for Phone A to open room…"

Phone A must have created the room (Setup) before the TV link is opened; the TV retries automatically every few seconds. Check the room code in the link matches. If the TV's browser can't connect at all, it may not support WebRTC — use a laptop/streaming stick browser or screen mirroring instead.

No sound on the TV

Browsers only allow sound after a click — click once on the TV page (or press OK on the remote). Tick Also play sound on this device to hear it on Phone A too.

Testing without tracking

Tap or click the picture in the Duck shoot tab to fire at that spot.

10e. Theremin

Press ▶ Play while standing facing Phone A — the instrument is placed around where you stand (Re-centre moves it). Classic: move your right hand toward an invisible antenna to your front-right for higher notes; raise your left hand above an invisible loop at your front-left for more volume (left hand down = silent). Simple: right-hand height is pitch, left-hand height is volume. One-hand play fixes the volume so only the right hand is needed.

No sound

Tap Play again (browsers need a tap to start audio); check the device isn't on silent and the Volume slider is up. Your volume hand must be raised (or tick One-hand play). The picture shows "Raise your left hand to play" when volume is zero.

Notes jump around / hard to hit

Choose a scale (Major or Pentatonic) instead of Continuous, set Glide to Smooth, narrow the Range to 1 octave, and use Simple mode. Good tracking helps: hands visible to two phones, decent light.

Wrong octave or key

Change Key and Lowest octave. The staff on the left shows which notes you can reach.

Recording

⏺ Record saves what you play as an audio file (WebM or M4A depending on the browser). Tap again to stop and save.

10e-2. Drums

An air-drum kit appears around you: snare in front, hi-hat on your left, two toms ahead, floor tom on your right, crash cymbal up on the left and ride cymbal up on the right, with the bass drum on the floor. Play it like a real kit, standing up.

  1. Open Drums, stand where you want to play facing Phone A, and press 🥁 Play drums. Stand still for a moment — the kit is placed around you.
  2. Hit downwards through a drum or cymbal. The sound plays as your hand passes down through its surface; harder hits are louder. A blue (left) or pink (right) dot on a drum shows which one your hand is over.
  3. Stamp your right foot for the bass drum: lift it, then bring it down.
  4. Lift your left foot to open the hi-hat (a longer, splashier sound); put it down for a closed "chick".
SettingWhat it does
Re-centre kitMoves the kit to where you're standing now (stand still for a second).
Kit height / Kit sizeRaise or lower everything (±25 cm), or spread it out / bring it closer (80–130%). Make it smaller for children.
Hit sensitivityHow fast a stroke has to be to count: light taps, normal, or big strokes only (fewer accidental hits).
Early triggerCamera tracking runs about a tenth of a second behind you. Early trigger watches how fast your hand is falling and plays the sound just before it reaches the drum, which hides most of the delay. Turn it down if hits sound early.
Bass drum / Hi-hat pedalWhich foot plays the bass drum (right, left, either, or off) and whether the left foot works the hi-hat.
DrummerWhich character plays — the skeleton, or any costume.
MetronomeA click (accent on beat 1) at 50–200 beats per minute, with a flashing light.
Record / Play back / LoopRecord a beat, play it back (the drums light up as it plays) and drum along with yourself; Loop repeats it.
ViewBehind you (like sitting at the kit) or audience (from the front).

In demo mode the simulated person plays a rock groove with a fill, so you can hear and see the whole kit without cameras. For a whole song, tap ▶ Watch a drum performance: it loads the built-in drummer movement file, starts the demo and the drums, and the drummer plays a 48-second performance on a loop. The kit is placed once the drummer has stood still for a moment (facing is taken from the hips, which don't twist while drumming).

Drums don't sound when I hit them

Strike down through the drum, not sideways, and make sure the dot shows you're over the right one. Try Light taps sensitivity. Keep both hands in all the cameras' views. Turn the sound up (and check silent mode on iPhone).

Drums play on their own, or twice

Choose Big strokes only, or reduce Early trigger. Jittery tracking (poor lighting, hands at the edge of a camera) causes false hits — improve the setup check first.

I can't reach the cymbals / the drums are too low

Use Kit height and Kit size, and check your height is right in Setup (the kit is sized from it). Re-centre after moving.

The bass drum doesn't play

Lift your foot clearly (10 cm or more) and stamp down. Feet near the bottom edge of a camera's picture track badly — move the phones back.

10g. Couch control & gestures

  1. Calibrate pointing in Control → Pointing at the TV (5 targets, or Quick aim).
  2. Tick Turn on couch control.
  3. Hold up an open palm for about a second (or wave) — a menu of big tiles appears on the TV (or on this screen if no TV is connected).
  4. Point at a tile and pinch (or fire your finger gun), or just hover on it for 1.5 s. A fist with your other hand closes the menu; it also closes by itself after a few seconds.

Tiles: switch to any mode, next costume, start/stop the current game (or cycle the Playhouse, or record in the 3D view), close. On this device you can also just tap a tile; tap outside to close.

The Gestures card shows what each hand is doing right now — open palm, fist, point, pinch, peace, thumbs up, finger gun (cocked/fired), hand raised, wave — plus a bar per finger (green = straight). ▶ Demo gestures makes the simulated person cycle through hand shapes.

A gesture isn't recognised

Gestures need Body + detailed hands and your hand visible to at least two phones, ideally within 2.5 m. Watch the finger bars in the Gestures card: fingers you hold straight should be green, curled ones grey. If they're wrong, improve the angle/light — and run a tuning session so the thresholds can be adjusted for you.

The menu opens by accident

Couch control watches for a held open palm. Turn it off when not needed, or keep your hands relaxed rather than flat-open toward the cameras.

10h. Tuning session

A guided ~80-second recording that lets the tracking and gesture thresholds be tuned to your room, phones and hands. Run it on Phone A with all phones connected, cameras on (Body + detailed hands) and aligned: Control → Tuning session → ▶ Start tuning session. A voice (and the on-screen prompt) tells you what to do — stand still, walk around, then a series of hand shapes for each hand, finger-gun firing, and a wave. At the end a motionlab-tuning-….json file downloads: send it over for analysis. It contains only tracking points and labels — no video.

11. Speed, battery & heat

  • Tracking is demanding: keep devices plugged in for long sessions; they may warm up and slow down.
  • Body only is much faster than detailed hands.
  • Close other apps/tabs. Keep the site in the foreground.
  • Phone A does the most work (its own tracking plus all 3D maths and drawing); use your fastest device as Phone A, or a laptop.
  • The Sync line under the camera previews shows matched moments per second (aim for 8+) and network round-trip times.

12. Diagnostics & reporting a problem

  1. On Phone A, open Setup → Align cameras → Diagnostics — raw session recording.
  2. Tap ⏺ Start raw session, reproduce the problem (or walk around and use the games for 30–60 s), then tap ■ Stop + save raw session.
  3. A file named motionlab-raw-YYYY-MM-DDTHH-MM-SS.json downloads. Send it along with a description (devices, which phone was A/B/C, what went wrong).

The file contains every phone's 2D tracking points with timestamps, clock offsets, tracking mode, height, lens setting and the current alignment. It contains no video or images. From it, frame rates, timing, framing, lens fit and alignment quality can all be analysed offline.

Also useful: the alignment status text, the Sync line, each phone's setup-check line, and the Ping pong "Tracking delay".
